# EMG - Electromyography

Technique for evaluating and recording the electrical activity produced by skeletal muscles. ELectromyograph detects the electric potential generated by muscle cells and outputs electromyograms. EMG signals are used as a control signal for prosthetic devices such as prosthetic hands, arms and lower limbs. 

EMG studies on patients with cardiac pacemakers and implanted cardiac defibrillators should be performed carefully, not to interfere with the devices, but generally there are no issues. Though patients with lymphedema (or the risk of) are routinely cautioned to avoid percutaneous (through skin) procedures to avoid development of the condition or worsening. 

## Types of EMG

- surface EMG: Assesses muscle function by recording muscle activity from the surface above the muscle on the skin, needs at least a pair of electrodes (to record potential difference).
- intramuscular EMG: different type of needles exists for different purposes, but superficial muscle activity can contaminate the recording of deeper muscles. First resting activity is assessed and later the procedure moves onto voluntary contractions. The electrode has to be placed at various locations to obtain an accurate study. 

## EMG signal decomposition

Decomposing signals into their constituent MUAPs (motor unit action potential - sum of all electrical activity, from neuron till muscle fibre)

## EMG signal processing

Translation of the raw signal into single polarity (usually positive) to avoid null average. 

- full wave rectification (preferred method): equivalent to taking the absolute value of the signal
- half wave rectification: discarding the signals below baseline 

## Other uses

- wearable cockpit for pilots 
- silent speech recognition (thtrough observing muscles associated with speech)
